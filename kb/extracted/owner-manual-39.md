## Stick Weld Penetration (Workpiece Heat Control)

### FIGURE: stick-weld-penetration-profile-views
**Caption:** PROFILE VIEWS
**Type:** diagram
**Description:** A row of three side-by-side cross-section (profile view) diagrams of a workpiece joint, each showing a different weld penetration outcome. Each diagram shows a gray rectangular workpiece cross-section with a darker gray weld bead shape cutting into/sitting on top of it.
- Left diagram, labeled "EXCESS PENETRATION OR BURN-THROUGH — Weld droops on top and underneath or falls through entirely, making a hole." Shows the dark weld material sagging deeply through the full thickness of the workpiece, bulging out both the top and bottom surfaces (indicating burn-through/a hole).
- Center diagram, labeled "PROPER PENETRATION — Weld is visible underneath and bulges slightly on top." Shows the dark weld material filling the joint with a slight bulge on the top surface and a small, controlled bulge visible on the underside — the correct/ideal weld profile.
- Right diagram, labeled "INADEQUATE PENETRATION — Weld does not contact the joint fully, just on the surface." Shows the dark weld material sitting only on the top surface of a two-piece joint, not penetrating down into the gap between the two workpiece halves.
No numbered callouts are used; the three panels are distinguished by their text headers above each drawing. Caption "PROFILE VIEWS" appears centered beneath the middle diagram.

### POSSIBLE CAUSES AND SOLUTIONS FOR EXCESS PENETRATION OR BURN-THROUGH
1. **Workpiece overheating:** Reduce current.
2. **Welding speed too slow:** Increase welding speed and ensure that welding speed is kept steady.

### POSSIBLE CAUSES AND SOLUTIONS FOR INADEQUATE PENETRATION
1. **Incorrect welding technique:** Keep arc on leading edge of weld puddle. Hold torch at proper angles.
2. **Insufficient weld heat:** Slow down so fill material has time to melt into the weld location. Increase current.
3. **Workpieces too thick/close:** Bevel thick workpieces, allow slight gap, and weld in several passes.
4. **Insufficient weld material:** Increase amount of fill material.

## Stick Weld – Weld Not Adhering Properly

Gaps present between weld and previous bead or between weld and workpiece. See areas below.

These circled/arrowed areas illustrate where gaps occur "between weld and previous bead or between weld and workpiece" as described in the page text. No numeric callouts are used — the dashed circles serve as the visual pointers referenced by "See areas below."
**Answers questions like:**
- Where do gaps typically form when a stick weld doesn't adhere properly?
- What does a poorly adhered stick weld look like in cross-section?
- What areas of the weld bead should I inspect for adhesion gaps?

### POSSIBLE CAUSES AND SOLUTIONS
1. **Incorrect welding technique:** Place stringer bead at correct place in joint. Adjust workpiece position or weld angle to permit proper welding to bottom of piece. Keep arc on leading edge of weld puddle. Hold Electrode and fill material at proper angles.
2. **Insufficient weld heat:** Increase current.
3. **Dirty workpiece:** Clean workpiece down to bare metal.
4. **Insufficient weld material:** Increase amount of fill material.
5. **Distance between workpieces to large:** Decrease distance and increase bevel.

## Stick Weld – Bend at Joint

### FIGURE: stick-weld-bend-at-joint-profile-view
**Caption:** PROFILE VIEW
**Type:** diagram
**Description:** A single cross-section (profile view) diagram showing two workpiece pieces meeting at an angled/bent joint, with a dark weld bead deposited at the joint between them. The diagram illustrates the workpieces bending/angling away from each other at the weld location rather than remaining flat/coplanar, which is the defect being discussed ("Bend at Joint"). No numbered callouts are present.
**Answers questions like:**
- What causes workpieces to bend at the weld joint during stick welding?
- What does a "bend at joint" defect look like?
- How do I prevent workpieces from warping/bending while stick welding?

### POSSIBLE CAUSES AND SOLUTIONS
1. **Improper clamping:** Clamp workpieces securely. Make tack welds to hold workpieces.
2. **Excessive heat:** Weld a small portion and allow to cool before proceeding. Increase weld speed.
