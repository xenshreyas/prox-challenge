### FIGURE: inadequate-penetration-profile
**Caption:** INADEQUATE PENETRATION — Weld does not penetrate the joint fully, just on the surface.
**Type:** diagram
**Description:** A profile-view (cross-section) diagram of two gray workpiece blocks with a shallow weld bead sitting only on the surface of the joint, not fusing down into the gap between the two pieces. An arrow points down and to the left toward the "POSSIBLE CAUSES AND SOLUTIONS" list below it (numbered items 4–7).
**Answers questions like:** What does inadequate weld penetration look like? Why is my weld only sitting on the surface? What causes shallow, surface-only welds? How do I fix insufficient weld penetration?

**POSSIBLE CAUSES AND SOLUTIONS** (for Excess Penetration / Burn-Through)
1. **Workpiece overheating:**
   Reduce wire feed speed.
   Decrease weld current.
2. **Travel speed too slow:**
   Increase travel speed and ensure that travel speed is kept steady.
3. **Excessive material at weld:**
   Reduce wire feed speed.

**POSSIBLE CAUSES AND SOLUTIONS** (for Inadequate Penetration)
4. **Incorrect welding technique:**
   Maintain 1/2" or less CTWD.
   Keep arc on leading edge of weld puddle.
   Hold MIG Gun at proper angles.
5. **Insufficient weld heat:**
   Reduce travel speed.
   Increase weld current.
6. **Workpieces too thick/close:**
   Bevel thick workpieces, allow slight gap, and weld in several passes.
7. **Insufficient weld material:**
   Increase wire feed speed.

## Bend at Joint

### FIGURE: bend-at-joint-profile
**Caption:** PROFILE VIEW
**Type:** diagram
**Description:** A profile-view (cross-section) diagram of two gray workpiece blocks joined at an angled (bent) joint, with a dark weld bead filling the V-shaped gap between the angled pieces. Illustrates a welded joint that has a bend rather than a flat orientation.
**Answers questions like:** What does a welded bend joint look like in cross-section? How is a weld bead shaped when joining two angled workpieces? What is a "bend at joint" weld?

**POSSIBLE CAUSES AND SOLUTIONS**
1. **Improper clamping:**
   Clamp workpieces securely.
   Make tack welds to hold workpieces.
2. **Excessive heat:**
   Weld a small portion and allow to cool before proceeding.
   Increase travel speed.
   Reduce wire feed speed.

### FIGURE: slag-over-weld-top-view
**Caption:** TOP VIEW — PARTIALLY CHIPPED AWAY TO SHOW WELD
**Type:** photo

Part of the slag coating has been chipped away to reveal the shiny weld metal underneath, showing the contrast between the slag crust and the actual weld bead.
**Answers questions like:** What does slag look like on top of a flux-cored weld? How do I know if slag has been properly chipped away? What does a weld bead look like underneath the slag? Why does my flux-cored weld have a dark crust on it?

Slag is a necessary part of a flux-cored wire weld. It shields the weld from impurities. Clean off the slag with a Chipping Hammer and Wire Brush after welding.

Gas-shielded MIG welds are protected by the shielding gas and do not need slag to protect them.

## Weld Not Adhering Properly

Gaps present between weld and previous bead or between weld and workpiece. See areas below.

### FIGURE: weld-not-adhering-profile
**Caption:** PROFILE VIEW
**Type:** diagram
**Description:** A profile-view (cross-section) diagram of two gray workpiece blocks joined by a weld bead, with dashed white circles highlighting three specific gap locations: two circles at the upper left and upper right where the weld meets the top surface of each workpiece, and one circle at the bottom center where the weld root meets the joint gap. These circled areas indicate where gaps/lack of fusion commonly occur between the weld and the base material or previous bead.
**Answers questions like:** Where do gaps typically form when a weld isn't adhering properly? What areas of a weld cross-section should I inspect for poor fusion? What does lack of fusion look like between a weld and the workpiece? Where should I check for gaps between weld beads?

**POSSIBLE CAUSES AND SOLUTIONS**
1. **Incorrect welding technique:**
   Place stringer bead at correct place in joint.
   Adjust workpiece position or weld angle to permit proper welding to bottom of piece.
   Pause briefly at sides during weave bead.
   Keep arc on leading edge of weld puddle.
   Hold MIG Gun at proper angles.
2. **Insufficient weld heat:**
   Increase current.
   Increase wire feed speed.
3. **Dirty workpiece:**
   Clean workpiece down to bare metal.
4. **Insufficient weld material:**
   Increase wire feed speed.
5. **Workpiece gap too narrow:**
   Widen groove or increase bevel.
