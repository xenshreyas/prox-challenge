# Stick Weld Diagnosis – Workpiece Heat Control / Weld Penetration

### FIGURE: penetration-heat-diagnosis-diagram
**Caption:** none
**Type:** diagram
**Description:** A diagram with three cross-section illustrations of a weld bead in a workpiece, arranged left to right:
1. "INADEQUATE PENETRATION" – shows a shallow weld bead sitting on top of a joint gap, with the joint not fused/filled at the root (a visible gap remains between the two plates below the bead).
2. "PROPER PENETRATION" – shows a weld bead that fully fills the joint with a smooth tapered penetration profile reaching just through the joint.
3. "EXCESS PENETRATION OR BURN-THROUGH" – shows a weld bead where the penetration profile bulges through and below the workpiece, indicating the weld has blown through the back side of the joint.

Below the three illustrations is a horizontal double-headed arrow bar (black, spanning the full width) labeled left to right: "Not hot enough" | "Ideal heat" | "Too hot" — indicating a heat spectrum that corresponds to the three penetration examples above.

Below the arrow bar are two columns of instructional text:
- Left column, heading "How to increase workpiece heat and increase penetration:" with subtext "(to weld THICKER workpieces properly)", listing: "a. Increase current." and "b. Weld more slowly."
- Right column, heading "How to reduce workpiece heat and limit penetration:" with subtext "(to weld THINNER workpieces properly)", listing: "c. Decrease current." and "d. Weld more quickly."

### FIGURE: example-stick-weld-diagrams
**Caption:** CLEAN WELDS FIRST! / Stick welds will have a coat of slag over them until cleaned.
**Type:** chart
**Description:** A row of seven side-by-side illustrated panels, each separated by vertical divider lines, showing a top-down cross-section sketch of a weld bead profile, a photo of the resulting weld bead surface, a bold label naming the weld condition, and (for six of the seven) a "TO CORRECT:" instruction. Some panels also include a small illustration of an electrode/torch angle and travel direction arrow relative to the workpiece.

Panel 1 — Label: "Good Weld". Cross-section shows a smooth, evenly domed bead profile. Photo shows a straight, evenly-rippled weld bead. No "TO CORRECT" text (this is the reference/ideal example) and no torch illustration.

Panel 2 — Label: "Current Too Low". Cross-section shows a small, narrow, rounded/peaked bead sitting high on the surface. Photo shows a narrow, high, ropey weld bead. TO CORRECT: "Increase the current." No torch illustration shown.

Panel 3 — Label: "Current Too High". Cross-section shows a wide, flat, spread-out bead. Photo shows a wide, irregular, flattened weld bead with heavy rippling/spatter appearance. TO CORRECT: "Decrease the current." No torch illustration shown.

Panel 4 — Label: "Weld Speed Too Fast". Cross-section shows a small, narrow, rounded bead similar to low current. Photo shows a narrow, wavy, thin bead. TO CORRECT: "Weld slower." Illustration below shows an electrode angled over the workpiece with a gray/light arrow pointing right, indicating fast travel direction.

Panel 5 — Label: "Weld Speed Too Slow". Cross-section shows a wide, rounded, oval bead. Photo shows a wide, heavily rippled bead. TO CORRECT: "Weld faster." Illustration below shows an electrode angled over the workpiece with a black arrow pointing right, indicating travel direction.

Panel 6 — Label: "Arc Length Too Short". Cross-section shows a small, narrow, flat/oval bead. Photo shows a narrow bead with heavy buildup/slag appearance. TO CORRECT: "Increase distance." Illustration below shows an electrode angled steeply with a black arrow pointing up-left/away from the workpiece, and dark slag/spatter marks on the surface.

Panel 7 — Label: "Arc Length Too Long". Cross-section shows a wide, rounded bead with irregular texture. Photo shows a wide, splattered, irregular bead. TO CORRECT: "Decrease distance." Illustration below shows an electrode angled with a black arrow pointing down toward the workpiece, and white/light spatter marks on the surface.
