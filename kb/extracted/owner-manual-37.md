# Welding Tips – Wire Weld Troubleshooting (Page 37)

## Wire Weld – Porosity

Small cavities or holes in the bead.

### FIGURE: wire-weld-porosity-top-view
**Caption:** Small cavities or holes in the bead.
**Type:** diagram
**Description:** A "TOP VIEW" illustration of a weld bead showing a wire feed cylinder/bead with numerous small white/light cavities or holes scattered along its surface, illustrating porosity defects. No numbered callouts; labeled only "TOP VIEW".
**Answers questions like:** What does porosity look like in a wire weld? What is a porosity defect? How can I visually identify porosity in a MIG weld bead? What does a top view of a porous weld bead look like?

POSSIBLE CAUSES AND SOLUTIONS
1. **Incorrect polarity:**
   Check that polarity is set correctly for type of welding.
2. **Insufficient shielding gas (MIG only):**
   Increase flow of gas.
   Clean nozzle.
   Maintain proper CTWD.
3. **Incorrect shielding gas (MIG only):**
   Use shielding gas recommended by wire supplier.
4. **Dirty workpiece or welding wire:**
   Clean workpiece down to bare metal.
   Make certain that wire is clean and free from oil, coatings, and other residues.
5. **Inconsistent travel speed:**
   Maintain steady travel speed.
6. **CTWD too long:**
   Reduce CTWD.

## Wire Weld – Excessive Spatter

Fine spatter is normal.
Spatter that is grainy and large is a problem.

POSSIBLE CAUSES AND SOLUTIONS
1. **Dirty workpiece or welding wire:**
   Clean workpiece down to bare metal.
   Make certain that wire is clean and free from oil, coatings, and other residues.
2. **Incorrect polarity:**
   Check that polarity is set correctly for type of welding.
3. **Insufficient shielding gas (MIG only):**
   Increase flow of gas.
   Clean nozzle.
   Maintain proper CTWD.
4. **Wire feeding too fast:**
   Reduce wire feed speed.
5. **CTWD too long:**
   Reduce CTWD.

## Wire Weld – Burn-Through

Base material melts away, leaving a hole in the weld.

### FIGURE: wire-weld-burn-through-top-view
**Caption:** Base material melts away, leaving a hole in the weld.
**Type:** diagram
**Description:** A "TOP VIEW" illustration of a weld bead cylinder with a large white/empty oval-shaped hole punched through the middle of the ribbed bead, representing base material burn-through. No numbered callouts; labeled only "TOP VIEW".
**Answers questions like:** What does burn-through look like in a wire weld? What causes a hole to form in the base material during welding? How can I identify burn-through visually? What top-view appearance indicates burn-through damage?

POSSIBLE CAUSES AND SOLUTIONS
1. **Workpiece overheating:**
   Reduce current and/or wire feed speed.
2. **Travel speed too slow:**
   Increase travel speed and ensure that travel speed is kept steady.
3. **Excessive material at weld:**
   Reduce wire feed speed.
